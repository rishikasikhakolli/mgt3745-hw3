## 1. Candidate Problem  

*Traveling is one of people's most beloved hobbies, and is a time where they are constantly taking pictures to share with the world. However, in this day and age, social media is more judgemental of the content you post. Certain platforms are too formal, while others are too serious. For example, I take trips often and love sharing about them with my friends on social and text, but I never get to simply put my thoughts out there in a quick way and often forget details since they were never recorded.*

---

## 2. Wickedness Test  

**• Not easily possible to formulate a problem statement:** *The issue arrises from different perspectives and is not clear what the actual problem is: casual vs serious sharing and recommendation bias from review sites.*

**• No stopping rule:** *Traveling can be something that is continued for your entire lifetime, so there is no end. Same with social media, you can continue posting forever.*

**• Solutions are good or bad:** *When a person posts a place or picture, it is never true or false, but a subjective opinion of it; same with a review being opinionated.*

**• Wicked problems may be a symptom of another problem:** *Travel apps are mostly places to review for others to see, there are ads and reviews that don't feel real and professional seeming photos that get rid of the social media feel.*

**Verdict:** *This is a wicked problem because travel preferences are subjective and platforms can only give opinions to a traveler without objectively solving it.*

---

## 3. Three Framings  

**Framing A (the obvious one):** *People don't have a casual place to upload pictures from traveling in a way that is extremely low stakes.*

**Framing B (stakeholder shift):** *Trip planners want authentic recommendations from friends or local small businesses, but can't easily get small, real recommendations without wading through crowded commercial review sites.*

**Framing C (goal shift):** *Travelers lack a low stakes way to capture memories and think about their trips for personal reflection, leaving their best travel moments buried in camera rolls or constrained to social media parameters.*

---

## 4. Commit and Justify  
`included in Framings`

**Chosen framing:** *B: Stakeholder Shift*

**Why:** *Framing B shifts focus to the friend planning a trip and the small businesses hoping for visitors. It turns personal travel logs into a trusted, low-pressure recommendation tool for friends, keeping data models (locations, photos, short reviews) distinct from the social layer.*

**What this framing makes invisible:** *By focusing on friend recommendations and discovery, it doesn't consider the purely private journaling aspect as much, nor does it cater to commercial review algorithms.*

---

## 5. Oath Check  

**The tool I already assumed:** *Beli*

**What would have to be true for it to be justified:** *The tool I assumed is related to food. A ranked-list setup like Beli's makes sense for travel when a user visits multiple spots in a specific city/category and needs a quick way to compare them. However, unlike dining out daily, travel is location-bound and periodic, so ranking is only justified if organized by city or category rather than global leaderboards.*

---

## 6. Adequacy Sketch  

*It would look like an application where people can choose a location in a search bar/map, input photos of that place, include images of a monument or activity, and review and rank the places against all the things they did there.*
